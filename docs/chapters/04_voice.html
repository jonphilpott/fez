FEZ has two sound sources, and they are not alike. The voice plays notes: it is gated, so it is silent until a note starts, and it fades after. The mod voice is always running, and does the slow, steady work of drones, noise and sweeps. Both feed the effects described in Space. Both are set by registers, so the editor, the CTRL list, a block and the matrix can all reach every knob below.

How the knobs are scaled

Every register holds 0 to 32767, but the things it controls are not that. A cutoff is a frequency, an attack is a time. FEZ spreads the 32768 steps exponentially, which is how ears hear: each equal step multiplies the frequency or the time by the same factor, instead of adding the same amount. So the bottom half of a cutoff knob is spent between 40 Hz and about 750 Hz, and the top half between 750 Hz and 14 kHz. Here is where five settings land:

The voice

The voice has four stages in a row: an oscillator, a wavefolder, a mixer and a lowpass gate.

The complex oscillator

An oscillator makes a repeating wave. The pitch of the note is how fast it repeats. FEZ's complex oscillator makes two waves at once, on the same pitch, and blends them:

  • a pure sine wave, which has no harmonics (no overtones above the note), and
  • a shaped wave, which is rich in harmonics. CXWAVE chooses it: the bottom third of the register is a triangle, the middle third a square, and the top third a saw.

CXBLEND mixes them. At 0 you hear only the sine; at full only the shaped wave. The blend keeps the loudness steady as you turn it, so nothing jumps.

The pitch is PITCH, in the units explained in Pitch: 256 to a semitone, with C4 at 15360.

Frequency modulation

A second oscillator, the mod oscillator, can push the pitch of the first up and down very fast. This is frequency modulation (FM). At low speed it adds a wobble or vibrato. At audio speed it makes the clanging, bell-like tones FM is known for. Three registers set it: MDPITCH is the speed, written as a pitch like PITCH (so C3 MDPITCH ! works; the lowest is about 8 Hz), MDWAVE is its wave shape (the quarters of the register give sine, triangle, square and saw), and FMAMT is the depth. The depth is squared, so the bottom of the register is subtle and the top is up to 2000 Hz of swing. The same oscillator is the sound of the mod voice, so retuning one retunes the other.

The wavefolder

A wavefolder takes a wave and, when it gets louder than a limit, bends it back on itself instead of cutting it off. That adds harmonics, and the louder the input the more the wave folds. FOLD sets how hard the oscillator is driven into the folder. Low values leave the sound close to the oscillator; high values make it bright and busy.

The mixer

Three things go into the mixer: the folded complex oscillator (MIXCX, full by default), the mod oscillator itself (MIXMD) and white noise (MIXNZ). White noise is a hiss with equal energy at every frequency. NZTONE is a lowpass on the noise: at the top it is open, and turning it down gives a softer rumble. The mixer has two more levels for the buffer device's output: MIXBP sends it into the lowpass gate, and MIXBF sends it to the effects, never gated. See Devices.

The lowpass gate

Next comes the part that gives the voice its character. A lowpass gate is a filter and a volume control in one, and the same signal drives both. When it is open, the sound passes at full level and bright. As it closes, the sound grows quieter and duller at once, as a plucked string or a struck bell does. It is modelled on the vactrol, a small lamp and light-sensitive resistor used in the lowpass gates of west-coast synthesisers. The lamp lights quickly and fades slowly, and the fade slows down the closer to dark it gets. That long tail is the sound.

You control it with five registers:

  • GATE: write any non-zero number and the lamp lights. 1 GATE ! plays a note.
  • GATELEVEL: how far the gate stays open all the time. The gate's real opening is GATELEVEL plus the lamp (the envelope), limited to fully open. At 0, the default, the gate is shut between notes and each GATE plays a note. At full the gate is always open and the voice drones, with no note needed. In between, the voice sounds softly all the time and each GATE lifts it. It is an ordinary register, so a matrix route (an LFO, say) can sway it, and a block can set it from code: 20000 GATELEVEL !. It is the way to hold a note open, on the handheld too.
  • ATK: how fast the gate opens, from 1 ms to 10 s.
  • REL: how long the tail lasts, from 10 ms to 4 s.
  • CUTOFF: how bright the sound is when the gate is fully open, from 40 Hz to 14 kHz. As the gate closes, the cutoff falls from here down to 40 Hz.
C3 PITCH !
2000 ATK !          \ a quick attack
28000 REL !         \ a long tail: about 1.7 seconds
26000 CUTOFF !      \ bright when open
1 GATE !            \ play the note

The envelope also exists as a number the matrix can use: ENV rises when the gate fires and falls as the sound fades. Route it to FMAMT and each note starts bright and mellows, which is how Glass Bells works. See the matrix.

A key held on the keyboard holds the gate open, and letting go lets the tail run. Blocks, pulsers and the sequencer fire the gate once and let it fall.

The mod voice

The mod voice plays the mod oscillator, the same one that makes the FM, through its own filter and its own volume, and is never gated. MDPITCH tunes it exactly as PITCH tunes the main voice: 256 to a semitone, C4 is 15360, and ST, HZ and note names all work. Its wave is MDWAVE. The oscillator and the noise are blended by MDNZ (0 is all oscillator, full is all noise). The blend goes through a ladder filter: a lowpass of the kind Moog made, whose steep slope gives a thick, warm roll-off. MDCUT sets where it starts to cut (40 Hz to 14 kHz, scaled like CUTOFF), and MDRES adds resonance, a boost at the cutoff that turns a noise sweep from a hush into a whistle. MDLVL is its volume: 0 is silent, which is why the mod voice does nothing until you raise it.

Low-passed white noise is quiet, so FEZ lifts the noise as the cutoff falls, up to six times, so a slow sweep keeps a steady loudness.

A2 MDPITCH !        \ the mod voice drones on A, under the main voice
12000 MDCUT !
20000 MDLVL !       \ bring the mod voice up

To make the mod oscillator follow the main voice, turn on MDTRACK. MDPITCH then means an interval above PITCH instead of a note of its own: C4 is the same note, G4 a fifth above, C5 an octave above, C3 an octave below, and CTRL shows it in semitones (+7st). It follows whatever the voice actually plays, glide, modulation and quantiser included, so it keeps step with the keys, the sequencer and your blocks. For FM this keeps the ratio between the two oscillators fixed, so the timbre stays the same from the bottom of the keyboard to the top; for the mod voice it is a second voice playing in parallel.

32767 MDTRACK !     \ follow the main voice
C5 MDPITCH !        \ an octave above it
5000 FMAMT !        \ FM at a 2:1 ratio: the same tone on every key

Older versions of FEZ set the mod oscillator's speed with a register called FMRATE. Patches saved by them load with MDPITCH at the same frequency, so they sound as they did, except that FM slower than about 8 Hz becomes 8 Hz.

The master level

LEVEL is the master volume, the first row of the CTRL tab. It comes last in the chain, after the effects and the compressor, so it scales the finished sound. If the output reaches full scale, the CLIP light in the status bar shows for a second.
