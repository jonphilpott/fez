This chapter is about the desktop app: what is on the screen, how to move around it, and which key does what. (The handheld has its own chapter.) FEZ has no mouse support. Everything is done from the keyboard, so that it works as well on a laptop in a dark room as on a small screen on a Raspberry Pi.

The window

The window is cut into tiles, and each tile shows one tab. The tile with the amber frame has the focus, which means it receives your keys. A layout is one way of cutting the window up; there are three, numbered 1, 2 and 3 like workspaces in a tiling window manager, and Ctrl+Tab moves to the next. Each tile starts on a tab that suits the layout, and you can change any tile's tab:

  • Layout 1: the editor on the left, the scope and the CTRL list on the right. For writing.
  • Layout 2: two tiles of equal width, the editor on the left and the CTRL list on the right. For writing and turning knobs side by side.
  • Layout 3: the scope along the top, the CTRL list and the devices below. For listening to a patch that plays itself.

Move the focus between tiles with Ctrl+Arrows. Change the tab in the focused tile with Ctrl+[ and Ctrl+]. Inside a tab, plain [ and ] move to the previous or next page: the next block in the editor, the next list in PATCHES, the next device in DEVICES. (This is why the editor cannot type square brackets: they are never needed, and the keys are worth more as page turners.) Alt works like Ctrl everywhere, for keyboards where Ctrl is awkward. Ctrl+= and Ctrl+- make the text bigger and smaller, Ctrl+F goes full screen, and Ctrl+Q quits. F1 shows every key on one page.

The status bar at the bottom shows the layout, the patch name, whether the pulsers are on, a short message about what you just did, and, at the right, the CPU load and the CLIP light. The CPU bar is the time the audio takes as a share of the time it has; green is under 70 percent, amber to 90 and red above. The thin marker on it is the worst moment in the last three seconds. Every example patch uses about 1 percent on an ordinary laptop. CLIP comes on for a second when the output reaches full scale.

The tabs

  • EDITOR: sixteen blocks of code. See below.
  • PATCHES: load, save and delete. See Patches.
  • CTRL: every register in one list, under group headings, starting with the master volume. Up and Down pick a row; Left and Right change it (Shift for big steps); type digits and press Enter to set an exact value. Tab switches between real units (Hz, seconds, percent) and raw register numbers.
  • DEVICES: pages for the delay, the cellular automaton, Lorenz, the quantiser and the Turing machine. Space does the page's main action (a step, a reseed, a freeze).
  • BUFFER: the tape, and its voices.
  • PULSERS: the four clocks. Space toggles one, F fires it, and you can type a new rate.
  • SEQ: the eight steps. Type a note name to set a step, and press Enter to clock the sequencer once.
  • MATRIX: the routes. Enter picks or adjusts a cell, and Backspace deletes a route.
  • KEYS: the computer keyboard as a piano (see below).
  • STICKS: only while a gamepad is plugged in. Its left stick plays the notes of a scale, and A, B, X and Y fire pulsers 0 to 3, exactly as on the handheld (see the STICKS screen).
  • SCOPE: a picture of the output wave, steadied by starting each sweep on a rising edge.

The editor

The editor holds sixteen blocks, one at a time. [ and ] move between them. Each block keeps its own text, and the text you are typing never touches the sound until you apply it. This is deliberate, so that a half-typed program cannot do anything.

  • Ctrl+Enter applies the block: it compiles the text and swaps it in. A mistake is shown in red with the line, and the word that failed is marked with a red block in the text. The old version keeps running. A fault that halted the block is cleared by applying it again.
  • Ctrl+R applies the block and runs it once, now. If it does not compile, nothing runs and the mistake is marked.
  • Ctrl+1 to Ctrl+4 switch on or off whether pulsers 0 to 3 run this block.
  • Tab completes the word you are typing, and cycles through the matches if there are several.
  • F3 opens the palette: every word and register, in ten lists (STACK, MATHS, CHANCE, LOGIC, MEMORY, FLOW, NOTES, NUMS, REGS under the CTRL headings, SCRATCH: S0 to S15 and P0 to P15), the same as on the handheld. Type the first letters of a word to find it in any list, or move with the arrows; Tab or [ ] change the list, Enter puts the word in at the cursor, Backspace takes back a letter, and Esc or F3 closes it. Ctrl keys work as usual while it is open. The box shows the help for the highlighted word (its stack picture and what it does, or what a register is for; a long one scrolls by itself), and it sits at the bottom of the editor, moving to the top when the cursor is low down.
  • Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y undo and redo. Ctrl+C, Ctrl+X and Ctrl+V copy, cut and paste.
  • There are no Home, End or Page keys: the arrows are enough for blocks of 128 slots at the most.

Making a sound

  • Ctrl+G fires the gate, once: one note.
  • Ctrl+P switches the four pulsers off and on together, remembering which were on.
  • Shift+Esc mutes and unmutes, with a 20 millisecond fade. The synth keeps running underneath. Plain Esc only cancels something you are typing.
  • Ctrl+T starts and stops a recording of the final output. See below.

The KEYS tab

To play FEZ as an instrument, go to the KEYS tab (Ctrl+] moves through the tabs). The computer keyboard now plays notes, laid out like a piano, by the position of each key so that it works on any national layout:

  2 3   5 6 7          upper octave: black keys
 Q W E R T Y U         upper octave: white keys
  S D   G H J          lower octave: black keys
 Z X C V B N M         lower octave: white keys

The notes are Z (C), S (C sharp), X (D), D (D sharp), C (E), V (F), G (F sharp), B (G), H (G sharp), N (A), J (A sharp) and M (B), and the upper row is the next octave, starting on Q. Space lets go of every key. Only this tab plays; the rest of the time the letters are for typing. If the window loses focus, every held note is released. How a key behaves is explained in the keyboard device.

A gamepad

Plug in a gamepad and the STICKS tab appears. Its two sticks are the modulation pots MP0 to MP3 on every tab (left stick left and right is MP0, up and down MP1; the right stick MP2 and MP3), and on the STICKS tab the left stick, or the D-pad, plays notes instead while A, B, X and Y fire the pulsers. The buttons go by the labels of an Xbox pad: A, B, X and Y are pulsers 0, 1, 2 and 3. Use the keyboard for the tab's settings. Unplug the pad and the tab leaves the tab bar.

Sound output and MIDI: F2

Press F2 for the setup page. It covers the window, like the help page, and F2 or Esc closes it. Up and Down move through everything on it. Your choices are saved in ~/FEZ/setup.txt as soon as you make them, and come back the next time FEZ starts. They are not part of any patch, because they describe your computer and your controllers, not a sound.

  • AUDIO OUTPUT: the sound devices. Enter on one sends the sound there; default follows whatever the system uses. If the saved device is missing when FEZ starts, for example because it is unplugged, the sound goes to the default instead.
  • MIDI INPUT: the MIDI devices. Enter on one listens to it, and none stops listening. Notes play the keyboard device, just as the KEYS tab does, so KBNOTE, KBGATE and the KBPUL pulsers follow them.
  • CHANNEL: Left and Right choose the MIDI channel FEZ hears, 1 to 16, or every one.
  • LAST MESSAGE: the most recent thing the input sent, such as CC 74 = 90 ch 1. Turn a knob and read its controller number here.
  • POTS: the sixteen modulation pots, MP0 to MP15, which the matrix can route anywhere. For each pot, choose which MIDI controller number (CC) moves it: Left and Right step it (with Shift, by ten), or type the number and press Enter. Below 0 is off. A bar shows where each pot is now.

Space on a pot changes how its controller is read, because knobs and endless encoders send different things:

  • abs: an ordinary knob or fader. It sends its position, 0 at the bottom to 127 at the top.
  • rel64: an endless encoder that sends 64 plus the number of clicks it turned, so 65 is one click up and 63 one click down. Some makers call this "binary offset".
  • rel2c: an endless encoder that sends 1 for one click up and 127 for one click down ("two's complement").

If an encoder makes its pot jump to the top or bottom, it is not in abs mode. If it moves the pot the wrong way, or only one way, try the other relative mode. The LAST MESSAGE line shows what it sends as you turn it. With an encoder, 128 clicks take a pot from bottom to top.

On macOS and Windows every MIDI input the system knows about is listed. On Linux FEZ lists ALSA's raw MIDI ports, which covers USB and hardware devices. A software port, such as a virtual keyboard, only appears there if it is connected to a virtual raw port; the snd-virmidi module provides those. If you plug a device in while the page is open, press R to look again.

Recording a take

Press Ctrl+T. The status bar shows REC, and FEZ writes what you hear, after the mute, to a 24-bit stereo WAV file in ~/FEZ/recordings/, named by date, time and patch, such as 2026-11-25_114302_Deep Loop.wav (the seconds keep two quick takes apart). Press Ctrl+T again to stop. The audio is passed to the disk by a separate thread, so recording cannot cause a glitch in the sound.
