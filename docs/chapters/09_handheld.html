FEZ runs on the TrimUI Brick, a pocket game console with a 1024 by 768 screen, a d-pad, four face buttons, four shoulder buttons and no keyboard. The same program runs on it: the buttons are translated into the keys the desktop uses, so everything described in the other chapters is there. This chapter explains how the buttons map to it, and how to put FEZ on the device.

The screen is drawn at 256 by 192 and scaled up by four, in large pixels, with the same colours as the desktop. It shows one screen at a time, instead of a window of tiles. The face buttons are laid out as on a Nintendo pad: A is the right one, B the bottom, X the top and Y the left.

Putting FEZ on the Brick

FEZ needs a launcher on the Brick that can start programs from the card; it was developed on NextUI, a replacement firmware, and the same folder layout works on MinUI. A program is a folder called a pak, with a script named launch.sh in it.

  1. Build the pak. On the computer, brick/build.sh cross-compiles FEZ for the Brick's ARM processor inside a container (it needs Docker, or Colima on a Mac) and leaves dist/FEZ.pak.
  2. Copy the folder FEZ.pak onto the card, into Tools/tg5040/.
  3. Put the card in the Brick. FEZ appears in the Tools menu. Start it.

FEZ keeps its files on the card in a folder called FEZ: patches in FEZ/patches/ and recordings in FEZ/recordings/. They are plain files, so you can copy them to a computer, or from one. If FEZ does not start, look at fez.log in the pak folder: it holds whatever the program printed.

The buttons that work everywhere

  • L1 and R1: the previous and next screen.
  • L2 and R2 (the lower shoulder buttons): the previous and next page on a screen, as [ and ] do on the desktop.
  • D-pad: move, and change values. Hold Y as well to take big steps, as Shift does on the desktop.
  • Start: fire the gate, to play one note.
  • Select: mute and unmute.
  • Volume + and -: the master volume.
  • Menu: open the menu (see below).
  • A, B and X: the three buttons whose job depends on the screen. A line at the bottom of the screen always says what they do.

The analogue sticks

The two sticks are knobs. Each direction of each stick is one of the first four modulation pots (see the matrix), on every screen but one: on the STICKS screen the left stick plays notes instead (see below).

  • Left stick: left and right is MP0, up and down is MP1.
  • Right stick: left and right is MP2, up and down is MP3.

At rest a stick holds its pot at the middle of the range, 16384. Push right or up and the pot rises towards 32767; push left or down and it falls towards 0. A small dead zone around the centre keeps a stick that does not quite return to zero from nudging the sound. The pots do nothing until you route them. To make the left stick open the filter, send MP0 to CUTOFF in the matrix:

MP0 CUTOFF 16384 ROUTE      \ left-right sweeps the cutoff

Loading a patch leaves the pots where they are, because they belong to your hands and not to the patch. The CTRL screen shows all sixteen under the POTS heading. MP4 to MP15 are not connected to anything yet.

Playing tunes: the STICKS screen

The STICKS screen turns the handheld into an instrument: the left stick or the D-pad plays notes, and A, B, X and Y fire pulsers 0 to 3, so the right hand can set off blocks, the sequencer or grains while the left plays. A pad for each button lights up when its pulser fires. The right stick stays a pair of pots.

The left stick's direction picks a note: straight up is the root, and each eighth of a turn clockwise is the next note of the scale, so the eight directions are the first eight notes. In a seven-note scale, up-left is the octave; in a five-note colour, the octave is down-left. The screen draws the eight notes in a ring (for colours, their degree numbers, 0 to 7), with the root in green and the one you are holding lit. While this screen is showing, the left stick does not modulate: MP0 and MP1 keep the values they had when you arrived, so anything routed from them holds steady while you play, and on another screen the stick sets them again the next time it moves.

The D-pad plays the same eight notes, for a handheld without sticks: its four directions and the four diagonals between them (press two at once) are the stick's eight directions. Pressing up and right together plays the second note, and sliding round from one direction to the next plays legato, without a new attack.

Because the D-pad plays here, it reaches the settings only while R2 is held. Five settings choose the notes and how they sound. Holding R2, Up and Down pick one, and Left and Right change it:

  • TYPE (STKTYPE): scales (0), the quantiser's eight, as the word SCALE gives them; or colours (1), the sixteen five-note scales in just intonation that the word COLOUR gives.
  • SCALE (STKSCALE): which scale (0 to 7, major by default) or which colour (0 to 15).
  • ROOT (STKROOT): the note straight up plays, C4 by default, as on the quantiser page. Left and right step a semitone.
  • MODE (STKMODE): how a note sounds, play or swell. The Menu's first tab can switch it too.
  • SWELL (STKSWELL): for the D-pad in swell mode, how long the gate takes to open fully, and to close again, in milliseconds: 50 to 4000, one second by default.

In play mode (0) the stick works like a key. Pushing it out plays the note with a fresh attack, moving round to another direction plays the next note, and letting the stick spring back lets go, so the release runs. Because it goes through the keyboard device, KBNOTE, KBGATE and the pulsers in KBPUL follow the stick as they follow keys. A colour's just notes fall between the keys: KBNOTE holds the nearest key, and PITCH the exact note.

In swell mode (1) the direction only moves PITCH, with no attack, and how far you push sets GATELEVEL, which holds the gate open by hand. Push gently for a quiet note, push to the edge for full volume, and let the stick return to fade it out. Turning the stick round while it is pushed out glides through the scale without a break. Swell mode owns GATELEVEL: leaving the screen or switching mode sets it back to 0.

The D-pad cannot tell how hard it is pressed, so in swell mode the time you hold it stands in for how far you push. While any direction is held the gate opens steadily, taking STKSWELL to open fully; let go and it closes over the same time. A tap barely opens it, a long hold blooms to full, and moving to another direction while holding changes the note without restarting the swell.

Near the border between two directions the stick keeps the note it has until you are clearly past the border, so a note does not flicker between two.

Writing code without a keyboard

With no keyboard, code is built by picking words from a palette. In the editor, press A. A grid of words opens. L2 and R2 switch the page of the palette:

  • STACK, MATHS, LOGIC, MEMORY and FLOW: the words of the language, grouped by what they do.
  • NOTES: C1 to B5.
  • NUMS: the digits 0 to 9 and a few common numbers. Digits stick together as you pick them (1, then 2, makes 12), and ENT ends the number. There is no minus sign: write 5 NEG.
  • REGS: every register, under its group heading.
  • PULSERS: the names P0 to P3.

Move round the grid with the d-pad. A puts the word at the cursor in your program, B closes the palette, X applies the block and Y deletes the last word. Spaces and line breaks are put in for you.

The menu

Press Menu for a box with two tabs. The first tab carries the name of the screen you are on (EDITOR, say) and lists the extra things that screen can do. MENU holds the things that belong to the whole machine. Left and Right (or L1 and R1) switch tabs, Up and Down pick, A chooses, and B closes the box (or, inside Go to screen, goes back one step). A screen with no extra actions, such as Scope, opens on the MENU tab.

  • Selecting and copying: in the editor, hold Y and use the D-pad to select text, as Shift does on the desktop. Then open the menu and choose Copy or Cut; move the cursor, even to another block, and choose Paste. B deletes the selection if there is one, and otherwise the last word.
  • screen's own tab: extra things the current screen can do that do not fit on A, B and X. On the editor these are run the block once, undo, redo, copy, cut, paste, clear the block, and four tick boxes that say which pulsers run this block. Other screens have theirs: on Buffer, trigger, stop, record, freeze and clear; on Devices, next and previous page and the reseed actions.
  • MENU tab, Go to screen: jump straight to any screen.
  • New blank patch: throw the current patch away and start empty. It takes you to the Patches screen and opens a question box: A yes, B no. Deleting or replacing a saved patch asks the same way.
  • Record to WAV: start or stop a recording.
  • Pulsers: switch them all off or on.
  • Button help: a list of these buttons, on the device.
  • Quit: leave FEZ and return to the launcher.

Saving a patch

On the Patches screen, press X. A name picker opens: Up and Down move through the letters one at a time, Left and Right by five. A adds the letter, B removes the last one, X saves, and Y gives up. The patch goes into FEZ/patches/ on the card. As on the desktop, nothing is saved automatically.

What the Brick does not do

  • There is no KEYS screen. The STICKS screen takes its place: the left stick plays the notes of a scale.
  • The handheld screens are smaller, so a few details, such as the longest register help lines, are shortened.
