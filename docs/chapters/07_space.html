This chapter is about the end of the signal path: the effects that make a sound last, and the buffer that records it. FEZ is built for slow music in the manner of Fripp and Eno, where a few notes are played into a tape loop and come round again, quieter and darker, for minutes. The effects and the buffer are the instruments for that.

The order matters. The voice, the mod voice and whatever the buffer plays back all go into the tape delay. Its output goes into the spring reverb, then a compressor, then the master LEVEL. The figure in the overview shows it.

The tape delay

A delay repeats a sound after a time. A tape delay does it with a loop of tape: each time round, the sound is copied back, a little duller and a little less steady. FEZ's delay keeps these faults on purpose.

  • DLYTIME: the length of the loop, from 0 to 12 seconds. The scale is a straight line, 0.37 ms for each step of the register, so 19930 is 7.3 seconds. The default, 814, is 0.3 seconds.
  • DLYFB: feedback, how much of the loop is fed back into itself. 0 gives one echo, and values near the top give echoes that go on for minutes. There is no cap: at the maximum a sound should neither die nor grow. Inside the loop, loud sounds are gently squashed so that it cannot run away.
  • DLYMIX: the balance of dry and wet. At 0, where a blank patch starts, you hear only the dry sound. As you raise it the dry fades as the echoes rise, and at full you hear only the loop.
  • DLYIN: how much of the new sound is written onto the loop. Full is the default. Lower values let new notes lie gently over the old ones instead of drowning them, and 0 closes the loop so that nothing new goes in. The scale is squared, so there is plenty of room at the quiet end.
  • DLYLP: a lowpass filter in the loop, 300 Hz to 12 kHz. Each trip round takes off more treble, so old sound fades to a murmur.
  • DLYWOB: wow and flutter, the slow and fast wavering of a tape's speed. It bends the pitch of the echoes a little.
  • DLYNZ: tape hiss, written into the loop. It adds a little more each trip.
  • DLYFRZ: freeze. At or above 16384 the loop stops listening and holds an exact copy of itself: no new sound, no hiss, no filtering. A frozen loop does not fade. You can play over it, and let go to return to normal.
19930 DLYTIME !     \ 7.3 seconds round
29000 DLYFB !       \ nearly 89 percent feedback
18000 DLYLP !       \ each trip round is darker
3000 DLYWOB !       \ a trace of wow
22000 DLYIN !       \ new notes lie over the old

The patch Deep Loop is built this way. To freeze it, go to the DELAY page of the DEVICES tab and press Space, or write 32767 DLYFRZ !.

The spring reverb

A reverb adds the sense of a room. A spring reverb was originally a coiled spring in a guitar amplifier, and has a bright, springy, slightly metallic ring. FEZ's is made of two short loops, one for each ear, tuned apart so that the left and right sounds differ.

  • SPRMIX: how much of the reverb you hear. A blank patch starts at 0, dry.
  • SPRDEC: how long it takes to die away.
  • SPRWID: the stereo width. At 0 both ears hear the same, and at full the two sides are as different as they can be, which makes the room seem wide.
  • SPRTONE: how metallic it is. At 0 each loop repeats one clean copy of the sound every few hundredths of a second, which rings like a tin can: the classic, boingy spring. Raising it smears each repeat into a spread of smaller ones, softens the pitches the spring rings at, and lets the loops drift a little so those pitches never settle. At full it is a soft wash. A blank patch starts halfway; patches saved before SPRTONE existed load at 0, the spring they were made with.

The compressor

Stacked loops can build up into something loud. A compressor turns down the loud parts, so the quiet parts can stay clear. FEZ's has no controls: it works above -10 dB, reduces the excess to an eighth, and reacts in 3 milliseconds. Its job is to keep a thick patch from overloading the output, not to change the sound. If the CLIP light still comes on, lower LEVEL.

The buffer

The delay is a loop of fixed length. The buffer is a store of recent sound that you can reach into at any point. It is a ring of up to 30 seconds (BUFMS sets the length, 50 ms to 30000 ms; the default is 2 seconds). A write head records into the ring, and when it reaches the end it wraps round and overwrites the oldest sound. Eight read voices play from the ring.

Recording

  • BWMODE: 0 means the head is frozen (it holds what it has), 1 means it records all the time, and 2 means a one-shot. The default is 1.
  • BWSRC: what the head hears. 0 is the mixer (the voice, the mod oscillator and noise, before the gate), 1 is the voice after the gate, 2 is the mod voice, and 3 is both of those together.
  • BIN: the record level.
  • BWFB: feedback from the read voices back into the head, so that sounds keep going round. It is soft clipped, so 100 percent is safe.
  • A one-shot records once from wherever the head is, for the length BWLEN (a proportion of the buffer, full by default), writing over what was there; anything past that length is kept, so takes can be layered. Write any non-zero number to BWTRIG to start one.

On the BUFFER tab, R records a one-shot, F freezes the head, S stops the voices, C clears the buffer and Space triggers the voices. The buffer audio is not saved with a patch, and loading a patch empties it.

Read voices

Each of the eight voices has five registers, numbered 0 to 7: GPOS (position), GRATE (rate), GLEN (length), GLVL (level) and GFLG (flags). A voice with no level is silent. A voice works in one of two ways, decided by its length.

A continuous voice (GLEN of 0) plays all the time. Its position is a proportion of the buffer, 0 to 32767. If the flag REL is set, it is a tap that sits that far behind the write head, which makes it a delay: 9830 GPOS0 ! with REL reads sound from nine seconds ago in a 30-second buffer. Without it, the voice is a free-running loop that starts at that position and plays round the ring at the speed GRATE sets.

A grain (GLEN above 0) plays a short snippet, that many milliseconds long, then stops. GTRIG is a mask of the voices to start, and the pulsers named in GRPUL start the next grain each time they fire, going round the voices in turn. A grain has a window, a fade in and out so there is no click: GWIN goes from a rectangle (with very short edges) to a smooth Hann curve. Many grains close together make a cloud of sound, which is how the Grain Cloud patch works.

GRATE is written as a pitch: C4 (15360) plays at the original speed, and each octave (3072) up doubles the speed and raises the pitch of the sound, within four octaves either way. The flags are added together: 1 REL (position is behind the head), 2 REV (play backwards), 4 TRK (follow the pitch of the voice, so the buffer can be played like an instrument) and 8 LOOP (a grain repeats until stopped, and GSTOP stops it).

The matrix can move every voice's position, speed and level (GPOS0 to GPOS7, GRATE0 to GRATE7, GLVL0 to GLVL7), so each grain can drift on its own: an LFO on one, the Lorenz attractor on another. Length and flags cannot be modulated.

Getting the sound back

The voices' sum is the buffer's output. MIXBF sends it to the effects, never gated. MIXBP sends it into the lowpass gate instead, so it is shaped by the same envelope as the voice.

A tape-loop patch, step by step

Here is the heart of Deep Loop. The voice plays rarely, softly and slowly. The buffer records the voice, and four read voices play it back from 9, 14, 21 and 27 seconds ago, which means that old phrases are fed back into the delay at distances that never line up.

\ the buffer records the voice, 30 seconds of it
30000 BUFMS !
1 BWSRC !

\ four taps behind the write head, quieter the further back
9830 GPOS0 !   1 GFLG0 !   9000 GLVL0 !     \ 9 seconds
15291 GPOS1 !  1 GFLG1 !   8000 GLVL1 !     \ 14 seconds
22937 GPOS2 !  1 GFLG2 !   7000 GLVL2 !     \ 21 seconds
29490 GPOS3 !  1 GFLG3 !   6000 GLVL3 !     \ 27 seconds
16000 MIXBF !                               \ send them on

\ the tape loop itself
19930 DLYTIME !  29000 DLYFB !  18000 DLYLP !  22000 DLYIN !

The voice, set to a long attack and release with a soft sine, is played by two pulsers a few seconds apart, each running a block that acts one time in three or four. Start from the example, and change one number at a time: DLYIN for how thickly the loop layers, DLYFB for how long it lasts, DLYLP for how dark it gets.
